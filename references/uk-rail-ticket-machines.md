## Taken

- People miss a small "touch to start" next to a bold tagline, and motion on the start screen makes it worse. The start instruction has to be the largest thing on a still screen.
- A confirm box that appears before people finish checking their details makes them abort. Confirmation must wait for the customer, never race them.
- Dark buttons on dark backgrounds and faint button edges cause wrong taps. Light, clearly edged buttons with strong text contrast work better, especially under glare.
- A review step before committing is reassuring. People said so in their own words.
- A related ticket-machine redesign found people touching their cards to the screen instead of the reader, fixed with arrows pointing to the reader: https://www.mt.design/portfolio/ticket-machine-redesign.

## Rejected

- Asking journey type inside a list of popular destinations. Mixing two decisions on one screen confused people.

## Not applicable here

- Station search, ticket types, and railcards have no counterpart at a snack-bar counter.
