## Taken

- The operation is a place on a line, and the same step is also a positive, negative, or zero result. Two representations of one change, then stop.
- The student is asked to predict the sign before the total is settled. The line is the place where that prediction is checked.
- Adding and subtracting are shown as opposites of each other, including that a number and its opposite meet at zero. The contrast is the point of the model.
- A change is answered by the model itself. Nothing else celebrates it.

## Rejected

- The net-worth story used as the way in. That context is theirs. The line is the model.
- Stop-watches, listed on the product family's own design page among the measuring tools. A clock is the wrong check for a fair puzzle.
- A sibling simulation whose description says to collect stars by playing the game. A prize is not a proof.
- Instant feedback that would skip a held prediction. The useful part is the prediction, then the result.
- Their name, mark, and any illustrated person on a related screen.

## Not applicable here

- Dragging, sliders, and radio buttons. A narrated film is not a simulation the child operates.
- Standards codes and grade filters.
